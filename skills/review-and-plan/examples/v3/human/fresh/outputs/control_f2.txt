Yes, add it. The log shows the same failure twice: CI failed on formatting on 2026-09-30 and again on 2026-10-18, and you fixed it by hand both times. A repeated manual fix is worth automating.

The cost is low. The Makefile already has a formatter command, so the hook only needs to call it. A local hook is also easy to remove, so if it turns out to be annoying you can delete it.

Two caveats:
- Two incidents is a small sample. It's enough to justify a cheap fix like this, but I wouldn't build anything more elaborate on it.
- A local hook only helps on machines where it's installed. It won't stop a formatting failure from a fresh clone, or from a commit made with `--no-verify`. CI remains the real check.

I'd keep the hook simple: run the Makefile formatter target, and either let it auto-fix or fail the commit. Auto-fixing is less annoying, but it can leave changes unstaged, so check how your formatter behaves before choosing.

If formatting failures show up in CI again after the hook is in, the cause is something the hook doesn't cover, such as another machine or a bypassed hook. At that point it would be worth looking at a CI-side auto-format step.
