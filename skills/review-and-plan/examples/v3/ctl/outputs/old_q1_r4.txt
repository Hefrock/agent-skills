Mode: quick. This is one design, but it is outward-facing (it closes issues across the whole org on day one), so I'm running the checklist rather than skipping it.

Coverage: I read the full design doc text you gave me and nothing else. I have no tools here, so I haven't seen your repos, your issue counts, your workflows, or how `gh issue close` is invoked. Every risk below traces to a sentence in the doc, or I label it as a general pattern I'm bringing in from outside.

1. Clarify. The doc leaves these open, and the answers change the risk:
- What counts as "activity"? Comments only, or also labels, edits, linked PRs, reactions, milestone changes?
- Does "every issue" include issues assigned to someone, or on a project board or milestone?
- Is there a dry run or a close comment, and is reopening expected to be easy?
I'd ask you these before building. The doc can't answer them.

2. Options (MECE, on the rollout and scope axis):
- A. As written: all repos, real closes, day one.
- B. Same logic, but report-only (dry run) org-wide first, then enable closes.
- C. Real closes on a small opt-in set of repos first, then widen.
- D. Don't automate closing. Only label issues `stale` and let humans close.
A, B and C differ on how much blast radius the first real run has. D removes automated closing entirely.

3. Premortem on A (imagine it failed):
- The first run closes the whole backlog at once. Source: "closes every issue with no activity for 60 days, across all repos" plus "enable for all repos on day one." Any repo with old, still-valid issues gets them all closed in a single night. Closing sends notifications to everyone subscribed, and the doc has no cap or batching.
- The exemption depends on a label that may not exist. Source: "Issues labeled `stale-exempt` are skipped." That is the only protection. Nobody knows to apply a label before the first run, and the doc says nothing about creating it in each repo or announcing it.
- "No activity" is undefined, so the job may close issues that are still live. Source: the doc never defines it. If it means "last updated" it may behave differently from "last comment."
- Silent failure or scope drift. Source: "across all repos in the org, using `gh issue close`." The doc doesn't say which token or permissions it uses. A missing permission could make the run fail on some repos. An over-broad token is a security concern.
- The #eng summary is the only record, and it may be unreadable. Source: "Posts a summary to #eng." After a mass close it could be hundreds of lines, and it doesn't say whether it lists closed issues per repo.
- Nothing in the doc covers undo. There is no reopen path, no close comment explaining why, and no rollback plan.
- General pattern, from my own background knowledge and not from your doc: stale bots that mass-close a backlog on first enable are a widely reported source of contributor and customer frustration. Treat that as weaker evidence than the doc-sourced items above.
I dropped other risks I could think of (rate limits, API outages) because nothing in the doc points to them.

4. Independent review. There are no subagents or other reviewer here, so this review was solo. It is weaker than a fresh agent choosing its own dimensions, and the risks above come from one mind that also framed the options.

5. Verify the mechanism. I can't do this here, and that is a gap, not a pass. Before relying on the design you should:
- Confirm how the job selects issues, since `gh issue close` closes a named issue and has no inactivity filter. The selection logic is the part that needs checking.
- Run it report-only against a real repo and compare the list to what you expect.
- Check the `stale-exempt` skip works by putting the label on a test issue and confirming it is left alone. Also prove the check can fail, by removing the label and seeing the issue get picked up.

6. Stop signals for the pre-build review: stop when (a) a second pass over the doc finds no new doc-sourced risks, or (b) the open questions in step 1 are answered. Don't keep iterating on the design on paper past that. The next evidence has to come from a dry run.

7. Needed twice. The doc doesn't say this need has come up before. If it's the first time, option D (label only, no auto-close) or B (report-only) costs little and leaves a record, so check the real demand before building the full closer.

Recommendation: don't ship A. Build B first, a report-only org-wide run that posts the would-close list to #eng, then enable real closes on a few repos (C) after the exemption label is announced and created. Tell me the answers to the step 1 questions and I'll tighten the plan.
